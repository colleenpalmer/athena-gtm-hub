2 · Context

Why this, why now

Parents aren't in our ICP today. The only trace is a backlog line in the drip plan ("Parent drip: low priority, activation risk") and a Meta ad set called Athena AI | Parents that never ran. We have no evidence either way, so this project exists to get some, cheaply.

There is a real upside. Our riskiest assumption is the student's willingness to pay ($5/month against a free stack). A parent who pays, or who simply sends the link, could remove that barrier. There is also a real risk: a parent can't connect Canvas or open a Study Space, so a parent-bought account the student never opens is the same activation problem we already see at Hinds.

Related: decisions/DECISION-LOG.md (Parent segment: test with a page first, 2026-10-06); work-in-progress/educator-segment/ is the closest precedent for how we test a new segment.

What the Meta export says Jan 1 to Aug 4, 2026

  • Parents ad set: inactive, $0 spend, no impressions. No data on parents at all.
  • Students | Sales: 123 trial starts for about $20,958, roughly $170 per trial start, against a $5/month product.

Source: docs/social analytics/metaAthena-AI-Campaigns-Jan-1-2026-Aug-4-2026.csv

So

Paid social is not the first test for parents. Start with owned and relational channels (see section 6), keep any paid test inside the ~$1k Bullseye convention, and don't run mass-scale paid before the product is validated (strategic priority #1).

3 · Who and what

Audience & goal

Audience

  • Who: Parents of college undergraduates (the student is the Persona 1 undergrad in reference/icp-and-personas.md).
  • Trigger: "My student uses ChatGPT for school. Is it doing their work for them?"
  • Wants: Their student to learn the material and do well, without hovering.
  • Entry criteria: To be sharpened with parent conversations (Q4). Candidates: parents of first-year students, parents of community college students.
  • Out of scope: Parents of minors or high schoolers (not today; COPPA and terms not reviewed). Parents who want to monitor their student.

Goal

One metric that matters: students who start a trial through a parent referral and then activate. Target and timeframe: to be set before launch (no parent baseline exists).

Assumptions: parents will share a link if it's easy and doesn't embarrass their student; the student, not the parent, completes sign-up; parent-referred students activate about as well as other D2C students.

4 · The story

Positioning

The direction in one line

The AI study tool that makes your student do the work. A low-commitment page that gives parents something good to send to their student, rather than something to buy or monitor.

GTM director

  • A parent "sign up" breaks the funnel: only the student can connect Canvas and activate. CTA is "send to your student."
  • Measure students activated via a parent referral, not parent signups.
  • Not a paid-first test: ~$170 per student trial start already.

Product marketing director

  • Parents are a third register: trust and integrity first, results second. No ZPD, no IgniteAI.
  • Lead with coach-don't-tell. It is the parent's actual fear and our sharpest differentiator.
  • Don't hide AI. For parents it's the anxiety; answer it in the first scroll.

Product design director

  • The student will read this page too. It must pass the gut-test over a student's shoulder.
  • One CTA above the fold; answer "what is it, is it safe, what does it cost" on the first screen.
  • Mobile first; parents arrive from a text, email forward, or social.

5 · What the page does

Page & funnel

Page structure

  1. Hero. Headline, one-line subhead, primary CTA, price line, "from Instructure, the company behind Canvas."
  2. Three-pillar strip. Coaches, doesn't do the work · built around their real classes · their account, their privacy.
  3. How it works, student-first. The student starts their own trial, connects Canvas if their school allows it, and gets coached. Needs a real screenshot or demo clip (the "watch, it just knows" moment).
  4. "Their account. Their privacy." The honest answer to "can I see what they do?"
  5. Parent FAQ. The table in section 4.
  6. Secondary CTA. Email capture for parent updates, followed by a short reply-to question set. This is the "get close to parents" lever.

9 · Voice and claims

Guardrails

Do

  • Say college students, coach to the answer, walk in ready.
  • Keep it calm and credible. Warm expert coach, not study buddy, not professor.
  • Answer the AI worry in the first scroll without making AI the headline.
  • Say plainly that it's the student's own account.
  • State the price plainly: $5 a month after a 7-day free trial; free if the school sponsors.
  • Write it so a student could read it over a parent's shoulder without cringing.
  • Flag proof gaps as "needs proof" (no efficacy stats, no testimonials).

Don't

  • Invent metrics, customers, quotes, or parent testimonials.
  • Imply parents can see, monitor, or get reports on their student. No dashboards, no progress reports, no dates.
  • Use fear or shame copy ("falling behind", urgency, grade panic).
  • Use banned words: unlock, empower, leverage, harness, future-proof; avoid actually, crush it, grind, hustle.
  • Say "FERPA compliant" or "FERPA certified."
  • Present Athena as part of Canvas, school-endorsed, or required for coursework.
  • Publish internal adoption numbers or invent discounts.
  • Promise that the Canvas connection works for every student; the school must opt in.
  • Address parents of minors or high schoolers.
