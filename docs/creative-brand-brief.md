## 1. Positioning

**What it is (internal one-liner):** A personal learning coach that knows your actual classes *and* knows you — so it tells you exactly what to work on next and coaches you to genuinely understand it, the fastest honest path to doing better in the courses you care about.

**The strategic promise (student-facing intent):** *"Do better in the classes that matter — because you actually get it, not just get past it."*

- **Headline benefit:** doing better / readiness (what students actually care about).
- **Hero job, stated sharply:** **points you to the right next thing to do** — orientation and prioritization powered by real course context (deadlines, materials, where you're shaky).
- **Engine / reason-to-believe:** real understanding + a rigorous knowledge model (active recall, mastery tracking, ZPD scaffolding). The *cause* of the result.
- **Method:** coaches you to the answer; won't hand over submission-ready work. Coaching *through* the work is the **delivery mechanism**, not a separate headline.

This is **not watered down**: results and understanding are *causally coupled*, not weighted equally to the same listener. The failure mode to avoid is pitching "grades" **and** "deep understanding" with equal weight to one audience.

### IS / IS NOT

| It IS | It IS NOT |
|---|---|
| A principled **coach** that earns its keep by being the most *efficient* path to real learning | A **spoon-feeding answer machine** / homework-doer (cede the cheat-seekers to other tools — on purpose) |
| Warm, credible, on the student's side | A **stodgy, annoying professor** lecturing that struggle is virtuous |
| Efficiency *toward* mastery → results | A **pedagogy lecture** — theory is the engine, never the student pitch |
| A named guide with the **student as hero** — the persona is the steady voice in their ear, not the star of the show | A **replacement for a human teacher** — it stands alongside the instructor, not in their place |
| For students who genuinely want to learn (incl. "I just need this prereq" *and* career-aligned deep-divers) | For everyone — a brand for everyone is for no one |

**Pedagogy register split (load-bearing):** The product's real learning science is the language of the *team* and the *institutional buyer*, **not** the student. Students hear *results + "no wasted effort"*; institutions hear *the learning science that produces them*. Same truth, two registers — this is how the hybrid model stays coherent.

**Category stance:** Independent, learner-owned study guide that is **LMS-native** and lives on the "learn, not cheat / learn, don't shortcut" side of the category.

## 2. Audience & job-to-be-done

**Business model:** hybrid. **Primary = D2C to students.** Growing second motion = **institutions buying seats** (primarily community colleges and undergraduate universities; some success in early experiments).

**Primary audience:** students who actually want to learn the material — a spectrum from *"I just need to pass this prerequisite / gen-ed"* to *"this is my career path and I really want to master it."* The brand must serve both without moralizing. Optimize for the formal higher-ed student; the working audience word is **students** (house glossary may say "learner" — either can survive, but the bullseye is not the lifelong learner).

**Future stretch:** high-school (K–12) students later. The brand must not feel so collegiate that it cannot grow down to a 16-year-old.

**The single job-to-be-done:** *"Tell me the right thing to work on next — for me — right now."* Direction and prioritization is the one job that, if nailed, makes the rest forgivable. Chat, quiz, flashcards, and similar modes are interchangeable means; orientation is the end.

**Why it's ownable — the precision intersection** (no competitor combines both):

- **Knows your *class*** — LMS-native auto-context: real courses, deadlines, upcoming exams become the study plan with zero upload/config. (Competitors in the upload-your-syllabus study-tool set make students paste in a syllabus.) Surfaced today as **Study Spaces.**
- **Knows *you*** — a rigorous knowledge/mastery model: *"five concepts for Tuesday, you've nailed three, let's drill the two weak ones."*

**Student-facing benefit (not the mechanism):** *"I won't waste a minute studying what I already know, and I'll walk in knowing I'm ready."* The mastery model is the reason-to-believe; *no wasted effort, walk in ready* is the benefit.

**Canvas = house advantage, not masterbrand:** Canvas is Instructure's LMS — we **lean into that relationship** as the signature way the product knows your classes (zero-friction context, institutional trust, the *"watch — it just knows"* demo). But the durable brand promise is still *"the tutor that knows you best"* — Canvas is the hero feature and campaign fuel, not the parent brand or identity frame. This keeps the consumer identity independent while we actively use the house relationship where it wins.

## 3. Personality & voice

**Archetype: the warm expert coach** — the brilliant person who makes *you* feel smart. Smart, approachable, calm, credible, fun to interact with; a step ahead but never showing off. You're *relieved* to have them because you never doubt their competence.

**Explicitly NOT the peer study-buddy** — the classmate who happens to be in your course and may or may not know the answer.

**Voice pillars (from the team workshop — validated):** approachable · authentic · **"humble brightness."**

**Aligned with the Instructure house voice** (clear · confident · conversational; knowledgeable-not-know-it-all, encouraging-not-patronizing, playful-not-juvenile).

**The celebration discipline — "applaud mastery, not attendance":** Keep streaks, the occasional emoji, and "learning moments" — but every celebration must be **specific, earned, and credible**, tied to *demonstrated competence*, not *activity* or *login frequency*. The instant it rewards attendance over competence, it has slid back into study-buddy.

**Delight calibration:** **mostly "it just worked" delight** (quiet, competent smoothness) with a **light sprinkle of personality** for engagement. When they conflict, **frictionless wins** — charm is seasoning, never the meal.

**Do / Don't**

- **Do:** be concise, warm, credible; explain *why* something's worth knowing; protect the learner's dignity; let a little productive friction happen when it's the right call.
- **Don't:** lecture pedagogy at students; be saccharine, hype, or "cringe"; nag; reward vanity metrics; ever talk down. House banned words apply (avoid *harness, unlock, leverage, empower, future-proof* → "future-ready").

**Workshop "not us":** *so edgy/wacky we're not taken seriously · cringe · bland · "more marketing, less tool"* (they want **"more tool, less marketing"**).

**Aesthetic throughline:** calm, crafted utility · supportive companion warmth · grown-up delight · deliberate escape from generic AI-slop · nothing that reads as enterprise LMS software or commodity answer-and-cheat tooling.

## 4. Emotional territory

**Dominant note: capability / earned progress** — *"I'm becoming better — a better learner, a better biologist / chemist / writer — through using it."* The brand makes you **more capable** and leaves you holding the credit. This is *transformation*, not "I got help."

**On first contact (priority order):** **relief** → **clarity** → **delight** (the "it just worked" kind). Relief is only *warranted* if it's earned by real, goal-aligned progress. Hollow reassurance with no capability behind it is the betrayal (the "AI slop" failure mode).

**The arc:** *relief* at first contact (*"it already knows me; I've got a competent guide; I'm going to be OK"*) → **capability / momentum** on the way out. The identity **anchors on capability**; relief and clarity are the on-ramp.

**FEEL (workshop, validated):** trust · relief · encouraged · "ok, not stressed" · guided · supported · safe (vulnerable, not judged) · "I'm smarter because of it" (confidence) · inspired/motivated · clarity · growing/progress · delight · respected.

**NEVER feel:** attacked · confused · lost · discouraged · bored · slowed down · afraid to use it · overwhelmed · condescended to · disrespected · "AI slop" · nagged · repetitive.

**Cardinal never-feel (third rail): "judged / made to feel dumb."** A little friction ("slowed down a bit") is acceptable — sometimes it's the coach doing its job — but indignity is never acceptable. **Principle: make them do the work; never make them feel small.** Emotional safety is the precondition for the Socratic method to work.

**Secondary cardinal never-feel: confused.** A confusing identity or experience betrays the core promise of orientation. If the product cannot tell you what's next, nothing else matters.

## 5. Brand architecture

**Two stacked decisions — both locked:**

### The product brand

The product brand is a **distinctive, ownable, consumer-grade identity** carrying the **"by Instructure"** endorsement (the brand guide's proximity rule makes endorsement non-negotiable), and described as **"powered by IgniteAI" in the institutional register only.** It behaves like a consumer product students download and recommend — not enterprise software they are assigned.

- **Not this:** a descriptive sub-brand tied to the house (e.g. "[House] Learner"-style) — legible to institutions but inert as a consumer brand; reads as a feature, not a product.
- **Not this:** an LMS-endorsed identity (e.g. "[LMS]-[X]") — institution-sold, teacher/admin-coded, and tethered to a single platform.

### The in-product coach

**One unified brand *is* both the product and the coach** the student addresses. The **incumbent persona is fully retired.** There is no separate character name, no learner-configured persona, and no split identity between "the app" and "the guide."

- **Why unified:** every interaction compounds into one brand. The student talks *to* the brand; the brand coaches *for* the student.
- **"Center the reader" reconciliation:** a *named coach in service of the student's progress* does **not** violate the house rule against software main-character syndrome. Main-character syndrome is when the *software* plays hero; here the **student is the protagonist** and the coach is the guide.

### Endorsement hierarchy

| Register | What shows up |
|---|---|
| **Student-facing (default)** | Product brand stands alone. *by Instructure* as endorsement. Canvas connection surfaced as feature, not identity. |
| **Institutional** | Full Canvas / IgniteAI / Instructure lean-in. *Powered by IgniteAI* available here. Credibility, trust, security, integration story. |
| **Campaign / acquisition** | Canvas-forward angles allowed (*"the study app for Canvas"*) as hooks — never as the masterbrand frame. |

## 6. Relationship to Canvas & the Instructure house — **stand apart, leverage the house**

**The shift:** We are no longer treating Canvas as something to downplay or distance from. Canvas is **Instructure's own LMS** — our parent company's core product — and we are **actively leveraging that relationship** as a strategic advantage: deeper integration, faster context, institutional trust, and a real data moat competitors cannot replicate. That leverage is intentional and increasing. It does **not** mean the consumer brand becomes a Canvas sub-brand.

- **Brand identity stands on its own feet:** The masterbrand is *"the tutor that knows you best."* Canvas is **one powerful way it knows you** — the signature way, for millions of students already on Instructure's platform — but it is a **hero feature and campaign angle**, not the brand's parent or identity frame. Think: *"the study app for Canvas"* works as a **campaign or acquisition hook**; it must never become the masterbrand or the lens through which students understand the product.
- **What Canvas gives us (and why we lean in):** Native course context without upload friction; institutional credibility at the point of sale; a distribution path through schools already on Canvas. This is the **"watch — it just *knows*"** demo moment. We own the relationship — this is house advantage, not a third-party integration story.
- **What stays separate:** The product brand is **consumer-grade and student-owned in feel** — downloaded, recommended, and remembered on its own terms. Canvas-native functionally; **never Canvas-*like*** in brand personality (no enterprise LMS software vibes).
- **On the student's side:** The brand belongs to the *student* — built by Instructure, deeply connected to Canvas when you're on it, but *yours.* Backed by the real privacy posture (read-only, student-owned, portable when you leave the school, no training on their data). This is the antidote to the "official school surveillance" dread some students feel toward institutional software.
- **Audience split:** *Institutions* get the full Canvas / IgniteAI / Instructure lean-in (credibility, trust, security, "works with what you already have"). *Students* get an independent brand that **happens to connect seamlessly** to the LMS they already use — not a brand that leads with "we're from your school's software vendor."
- **Identity principle:** The brand earns equity on its own — evoking the *benefit* (orientation, knowing you), not the integration partner. Canvas shows up in **campaigns, onboarding, and institutional register** — not as the defining frame of the consumer brand.

## 7. Brand expression & quality bar

**How the brand should feel across every touchpoint** — product UI, marketing, support, institutional sales, social:

- **Calm, crafted utility** — competent and frictionless before flashy. More tool, less marketing.
- **Supportive companion warmth** — present and human, never saccharine or cringe.
- **Grown-up delight** — specific, earned moments; not gamified attendance theater.
- **Anti-slop** — no generic AI aesthetics, no hollow hype, no "unlock your potential" energy.

**AI posture:** AI is the **engine**, never the **badge**. Do not lead with "AI-powered" in student-facing copy. The brand is a coach that works, not a technology demo.

**Solid > clever:** Prefer clear, credible expression over wordplay or trend-chasing. The brand earns meaning through the product experience — copy and creative should reinforce what the product already delivers, not try to carry it alone.

**The gut-test (use on any brand expression):** *Would a student feel solid — not cheap, not embarrassed — recommending this to a friend?* If the answer is no, it doesn't meet the bar regardless of how clever it is.

**What to avoid in expression:**

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| Enterprise LMS software vibes | Breaks the consumer-grade, student-owned feel |
| Commodity cheat/answer-tool energy | Wrong side of the category; attracts the wrong user |
| Pedagogy lectures in student register | Theory is for institutions; students hear results |
| Canvas as masterbrand frame | Canvas is hero feature + campaign fuel, not identity |
| AI as headline identity | Technology is reason-to-believe, not the promise |
| Celebrating attendance over mastery | Slides into study-buddy; breaks the coach archetype |
