# Audience

## Business model
Hybrid. **Primary = D2C to students.** Growing second motion = **institutions buying seats** (community colleges, undergrad universities). Future stretch: high school — the brand must not feel so collegiate it can't grow down to a 16-year-old.

## Primary persona: the capable-but-scattered undergrad (D2C)

- **Who:** Traditional undergrad, 18–22, first/second year, 4–5 Canvas courses in a demanding or pre-professional track (pre-nursing, psych, bio, engineering) where GPA gates something concrete — grad school, a scholarship, program admission.
- **Ability:** Capable, not struggling.
- **Context:** Works part-time; studies in fragmented pockets; phone as much as laptop.
- **Money:** Steeped in free-tool culture and price-sensitive — leans on free ChatGPT and Quizlet; will only pay for something that clearly beats the free stack at a moment that matters.
- **Top pains:** Can't see their own gaps (confuses "I've seen this" with "I know this"); no durable study system — intentions decay into cramming; material scattered across slides, notes, and Canvas; current tools solve only a slice.
- **Emotional baseline:** A low, persistent "am I missing something?" that spikes to panic 1–2 days before exams. Friction tolerance drops as stress rises.
- **Where they hang out:** Canvas (reflexively), ChatGPT, Quizlet, TikTok, Instagram, Reddit, Discord class chats, Notion/Google Docs.
- **Deliberately NOT for:** cheat-seekers wanting submission-ready answers (ceded on purpose); students who want a generic answer engine, not coaching.

## Secondary persona: the institutional sponsor

- **Who:** Success center lead, advising office, or Canvas admin; leadership sponsors the budget. Community colleges and undergrad universities. Must be on Canvas (disqualifier if not).
- **Trigger:** Students already using ungoverned AI; leadership asking for a stance; IP/privacy risk.
- **Cares about:** Security and privacy (read-only, no model training), Canvas integration, academic integrity, bounded cost, a partner they can call.
- **Common objections:** "Can students connect without our approval?" "How do we opt out / see who's connected?" "Is this FERPA compliant?" (careful — see legal no-gos in `voice-and-tone.md`).

## Jobs to be done

- **The single student JTBD:** *When I sit down to study, I want to be told the right thing to work on next — for me, right now — so I stop wasting time and walk in ready.*
- Late and stuck: coach me to the answer so I actually understand it, not copy it.
- Exams piling up: show me what I don't know so intentions don't decay into cramming.
- **Institution JTBD:** students are on ungoverned AI — give me a safe, sanctioned, Canvas-connected option without unbounded cost.

## Review guidance for directors

- Judge artifacts against the *capable-but-time-pressed undergrad*, not "students" generically.
- The register split is load-bearing: **students hear results + "no wasted effort"; institutions hear the learning science and safeguards.** Never pitch both with equal weight to one listener.
- The default objection is "why not free ChatGPT?" — every artifact should survive it.
- The emotional moment that matters: 11pm before an exam. Calm and credible wins; hype, shame, and urgency lose.
