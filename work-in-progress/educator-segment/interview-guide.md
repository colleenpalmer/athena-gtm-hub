# Educator Interview Guide

> **Project task:** Interview 5–10 target educators
> **Built from:** `icp-questions.md` (completed 2026-09-29)
> **Log results in:** `learnings.md`, one entry per interview, then a synthesis entry

---

## Why we're doing these interviews

**The hypothesis:** An educator will champion Athena and **sponsor it for their own students** ($5 per
monthly active user, averaged over the contract) without going through institutional procurement.

**The interviews exist to test our riskiest assumptions** before we spend anything on a channel test:

| # | Assumption | What would confirm it | What would kill or reshape it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Instructors will spend their own or departmental budget on a tool for their students | They've paid for student-facing tools or resources before, and they name a budget source without prompting | "I've never spent my own money on this," or "I'd need the dean to approve anything" |
| **A2** | They can use that budget without institutional sign-off | They describe a real path (P-card, discretionary funds, grant) they've used before | Every path runs through procurement or IT review |
| **A3** | Canvas admins will enable course-level access when an instructor asks | They know their admin and have asked for (and gotten) a Canvas change before | "I don't know who that is," or past requests took months |
| **A4** | Being a design partner is enough without an instructor dashboard | They're energized by shaping the tool, or care more about results than reports | They won't pay without seeing individual student usage |
| **A5** | Their pains match our draft: gaps surfacing too late, AI used without oversight, no 1:1 capacity, slow procurement | They bring these up *before* we mention them | Their real pains are different (a rethink of messaging, not necessarily a kill) |
| **A6** | Educator messaging needs to differ from student messaging | Their words and priorities differ clearly from students' | They describe value the same way students would (then no separate page is needed) |

## Who to interview

**Target: 8–10 interviews over ~3 weeks.** First exam results are coming in now, which is our primary
trigger, so recent pain will be fresh.

| Group | Count | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Hinds Community College faculty** | 3–4 | Their students already have Athena through institutional sponsorship. They show us how instructors think about, introduce, and judge Athena in real use. They **don't** test willingness to pay. |
| **Instructors at Canvas schools with no Athena relationship** | 5–6 | The real test of the sponsor hypothesis. Mix community colleges and 4-year schools. |

**Screening criteria (non-Hinds):**
- ✅ Teaches a first- or second-year, high-enrollment course that's mostly graded by exams (A&P, bio, chem, intro
  psych, stats, college algebra/precalc, or similar)
- ✅ School uses Canvas, and they post course materials there
- ✅ Full-time faculty, or anyone with access to some budget (department, grant, P-card)
- ✅ Not hostile to AI (neutral or curious is fine, and even useful)
- ❌ K–8; no Canvas; writing- or discussion-only courses

**Where to find them (our network first):**
- Hinds: coordinate through whoever owns the Hinds relationship before reaching out to faculty
- Instructure/CSM relationships at Canvas schools (ask CSMs for faculty contacts, not admins)
- Our own networks and referrals from each interview ("Who else should we talk to?")

## Before each call

- **30 minutes.** Two people if possible: one leads, one takes notes.
- Ask permission to record. Recording is optional; notes are enough.
- Look up their course in the course catalog so you can ask specific questions.
- Open the **interview notes template** (bottom of this file).

### Interviewing principles
- **Ask about the past, not the hypothetical.** "Tell me about the last time…" beats "Would you ever…?"
- **Let them name the pain first.** Don't mention our draft pains until the concept section.
- **Don't pitch until minute ~18.** The first half is about *their* world.
- **Listen for money and effort they've already spent.** Past spending predicts future spending better than opinions do.
- **End with a real ask.** A concrete next step shows interest far better than "sounds great!"

## The interview (30 minutes)

### 1. Warm-up and context (3 min)
1. Tell me about the course(s) you're teaching this term. How many students, and who are they?
2. How much of the course lives in Canvas?

### 2. Student struggle and support (7 min) → A5
3. Walk me through what happened after your first exam this term. How did students do?
4. When a student is struggling, when do you usually find out? What happens next?
5. What do you do today to support students outside class? (Office hours, tutoring referrals, review
   sessions.) What works, and what doesn't?
6. What's the hardest part of supporting [number] students at once?

### 3. AI in their course (4 min) → A5, A6
7. How are your students using AI in your course right now, as far as you know?
8. What's your stance on it? Do you have a policy in your syllabus?
9. Have you ever pointed students *to* a specific tool, AI or not? What was it, and why?

### 4. Budget and buying behavior (5 min) → A1, A2
10. Have you ever paid for something for your students yourself, like a tool, software, supplies,
    or review materials? Tell me about the last time.
    - *If yes:* Where did the money come from? Personal, department, P-card, grant? Who else was involved?
    - *If no:* What would it take? Who would you have to ask?
11. If you wanted to get a new tool for your class *this term*, what would that process look like?
    Who would say yes or no?

### 5. Canvas admin relationship (3 min) → A3
12. Have you ever asked your Canvas admin to turn on or change something for your course? How did
    that go, and how long did it take?
13. Do you know who your Canvas admin is?

### 6. Concept reaction (6 min) → A1, A4, A6

**Show:** a 2-minute demo of Athena tutoring on real course material (coaching, not answering).

**Then explain the offer in plain terms:**
> "Some instructors want to give this to their whole class. The way that works: you sponsor your
> students, it's free for them, and you pay $5 per student who actually uses it each month. You decide
> how to introduce it. Students sign up and connect their Canvas account. Your Canvas admin turns on the
> connection for your course, and we help you with that step. We don't have an instructor view yet.
> Instructors who sponsor early would help us design what instructors see, while keeping students'
> privacy protected."

14. What's your honest first reaction?
15. What would you need to see or know before doing this for your class?
16. Paying per student who actually uses it: how does that land for you?
17. We don't have an instructor view yet. How much does that matter? What would you want to see, and
    what would feel like too much, from a student-privacy standpoint?
18. How would you describe this to a colleague?

### 7. Wrap-up and the ask (2 min)
19. Who else should we talk to? (A colleague, or your Canvas admin)
20. **The real ask** (pick one that fits):
    - "Would you be open to us looping in your Canvas admin to see what enabling this would take?"
    - "Would you want to try this with one section next term?"
    - "Could we follow up in a few weeks once we've talked to more instructors?"

**Hinds-only swap (replace sections 4 and 6):**
- Have you mentioned Athena to your students? Why or why not? How did you frame it?
- Have you noticed any difference, or heard anything from students?
- What would make you actively recommend it?
- If the school *weren't* covering it, would you consider sponsoring your own class? What would hold you back?

## What we can and can't say

| ✅ Can say | ❌ Don't say |
|---|---|
| $5 per monthly active user, averaged over the contract (public) | Any discount, annual deal, or custom rate |
| Under a sponsorship, Instructure acts as a FERPA-designated school official | "FERPA compliant" or "FERPA certified" |
| No instructor view yet; we want to design it *with* instructors, protecting student privacy | Any promised dashboard, feature, or date |
| Athena coaches instead of answering; won't produce submission-ready work; can't see unpublished quiz questions | That Athena is part of Canvas, endorsed by their school, or required for coursework |
| Course-level enablement goes through their Canvas admin, and we'll help | That the instructor can turn it on themselves |
| Hinds CC offers Athena to all students this fall | Internal adoption numbers |
| — | Coupons for non-Canvas schools (possible future, not an offer) |

**Proposed decision rule (team to confirm):**
- **Proceed to a sponsorship test** if at least **3 non-Hinds instructors** agree to a concrete next step
  (an intro to their admin, a trial with one section, or checking a budget source) *and* A1 and A2 aren't

- **Pause the segment** if instructors show little pain *and* little willingness to spend.
