Educator Interview Guide

Hypothesis: An educator will champion Athena and sponsor it for their own students ($5 per monthly active user, averaged over the contract) without going through institutional procurement.
8–10 interviews ~3 weeks 30 min each Built from icp-questions.md (2026-09-29) Log in learnings.md
1 · Why we're doing this

What we're testing

We're testing our riskiest assumptions before spending anything on a channel test. Highlighted = riskiest.

A1

Instructors will spend their own or departmental budget on a tool for their students

ConfirmsThey've paid for student-facing tools or resources before and name a budget source without prompting
Kills / reshapes"I've never spent my own money on this" · "I'd need the dean to approve anything"
A2

They can use that budget without institutional sign-off

ConfirmsThey describe a real path (P-card, discretionary funds, grant) they've used before
Kills / reshapesEvery path runs through procurement or IT review
A3

Canvas admins will enable course-level access when an instructor asks

ConfirmsThey know their admin and have asked for (and gotten) a Canvas change before
Kills / reshapes"I don't know who that is" · past requests took months
A4

Being a design partner is enough without an instructor dashboard

ConfirmsThey're energized by shaping the tool, or care more about results than reports
Kills / reshapesThey won't pay without seeing individual student usage
A5

Their pains match our draft

Gaps surface too late · AI used without oversight · no 1:1 capacity · slow procurement

ConfirmsThey bring these up before we mention them
ReshapesTheir real pains are different (rethink the messaging, not necessarily a kill)
A6

Educator messaging needs to differ from student messaging

ConfirmsTheir words and priorities differ clearly from students'
ReshapesThey describe value the same way students would (no separate page needed)
2 · Sample

Who to interview

First exam results are coming in now, which is our primary trigger, so recent pain will be fresh.

3–4 interviews

Hinds Community College faculty

Their students already have Athena through institutional sponsorship. They show us how instructors think about, introduce, and judge Athena in real use.

Doesn't test willingness to pay.

5–6 interviews

Instructors at Canvas schools with no Athena relationship

The real test of the sponsor hypothesis. Mix community colleges and 4-year schools.

Screening criteria (non-Hinds)

  • Teaches a first- or second-year, high-enrollment course that's mostly graded by exams (A&P, bio, chem, intro psych, stats, college algebra/precalc)
  • School uses Canvas, and they post course materials there
  • Full-time faculty, or anyone with access to some budget (department, grant, P-card)
  • Not hostile to AI (neutral or curious is fine, and even useful)
  • K–8 · no Canvas · writing- or discussion-only courses

Where to find them (our network first)

  • Hinds: coordinate through whoever owns the Hinds relationship before reaching out to faculty
  • Instructure/CSM relationships at Canvas schools (ask CSMs for faculty contacts, not admins)
  • Our own networks and referrals from each interview: "Who else should we talk to?"

Recruiting emails

4 · Prep

Before each call

Logistics

  • 30 minutes. Two people if possible: one leads, one takes notes.
  • Ask permission to record. Recording is optional; notes are enough.
  • Look up their course in the course catalog so you can ask specific questions.
  • Open the notes template.

Interviewing principles

1
Ask about the past, not the hypothetical. "Tell me about the last time…" beats "Would you ever…?"
2
Let them name the pain first. Don't mention our draft pains until the concept section.
3
Don't pitch until minute ~18. The first half is about their world.
4
Listen for money and effort they've already spent. Past spending predicts future spending better than opinions do.
5
End with a real ask. A concrete next step shows interest far better than "sounds great!"

Hinds-only swap (replaces sections 4 and 6)

  • Have you mentioned Athena to your students? Why or why not? How did you frame it?
  • Have you noticed any difference, or heard anything from students?
  • What would make you actively recommend it?
  • If the school weren't covering it, would you consider sponsoring your own class? What would hold you back?
6 · Guardrails

What we can and can't say

✓ Can say
✕ Don't say
$5 per monthly active user, averaged over the contract (public)
Any discount, annual deal, or custom rate
Under a sponsorship, Instructure acts as a FERPA-designated school official
"FERPA compliant" or "FERPA certified"
No instructor view yet; we want to design it with instructors, protecting student privacy
Any promised dashboard, feature, or date
Athena coaches instead of answering; won't produce submission-ready work; can't see unpublished quiz questions
That Athena is part of Canvas, endorsed by their school, or required for coursework
Course-level enablement goes through their Canvas admin, and we'll help
That the instructor can turn it on themselves
Hinds CC offers Athena to all students this fall
Internal adoption numbers
Coupons for non-Canvas schools (possible future, not an offer)

8 · After all interviews

Synthesis & decision

Score each assumption across the non-Hinds interviews.

AssumptionStrong signalWeak / mixedAgainstVerdict
A1 Will spend budget
A2 Can spend without sign-off
A3 Admin will enable
A4 Design partner is enough
A5 Pains match
A6 Distinct messaging needed

Proposed decision rule Team to confirm

Proceed to a sponsorship test
At least 3 non-Hinds instructors agree to a concrete next step (an intro to their admin, a trial with one section, or checking a budget source) and A1 and A2 aren't clearly against us.
Reshape
The pains are strong but budget is the blocker. Revisit Scenario B (the instructor recommends Athena and students pay individually).
Pause the segment
Instructors show little pain and little willingness to spend.

Record the outcome in learnings.md and decisions/DECISION-LOG.md, then draft the persona with templates/icp-persona.md.
