2 · What's going on

What's going on

Product asked us for help getting more sign-ups in October: "would love some gtm collaboration on how to increase signups in Oct. they have a new in-person semester starting this week and online starting on October 19." This is about Hinds (confirmed October 5).

Hinds students can already sign up, and Hinds pays for it, so it's free for them. Our best guess is that many of them just don't know Athena exists or that it's free for them. The start of a semester is the best time to tell them, because that's when they open Canvas to find their classes.

This is separate from the plan for the emails we send after a student signs up (the Hinds onboarding emails plan). That plan is about getting students who signed up to actually use Athena. This project is about getting more students to sign up in the first place. They use the same analytics setup, and the follow-up emails matter more the more students sign up.

3 · How students get in

Three ways to sign up

A Hinds student can sign up in three different ways today. We aren't building anything new. We need to tell more students about Athena and send them to the easiest way.

Way to sign upWhat the student doesIs it free for them?What to know
1. Sign up on the main page and enter the school info Goes to the Athena sign-up page and enters the Hinds subscription information as part of signing up. I believe so, right away. Product needs to confirm. The student has to know Hinds covers it and find that option. (The onboarding emails plan calls this "Path A.")
2. Sign up normally, then verify later Signs up the regular way. Later goes to Subscriptions and clicks "Verify with Canvas." Only after they verify. Until then they're on the regular plan: 7 days free, then $5 a month. The most steps, and the easiest to forget. We don't know whether a student who never verifies gets charged $5 when the free week ends. That's question 4 in section 7. (The onboarding emails plan calls this "Path B.")
3. Click the Athena link inside Canvas Clicks a link in Canvas that opens a sign-up page similar to way 1. I believe so, right away. Product needs to confirm. The student is already logged in to Canvas, so this is probably the fewest steps. Our docs don't mention this link. They say Athena isn't installed into Canvas as an app. See question 2 in section 7.

My guess about what to do

Send students to way 3 (the link in Canvas) wherever we can show them where it is. Otherwise send them to way 1 with a link. Don't tell them to sign up first and verify later (way 2). It has the most steps and may charge students who forget. This is a guess, because we don't yet know how many students use each way or how many finish. That's the first thing to ask product.

What counts as success

The main number: how many new Hinds students sign up each week in October, compared with how many signed up each week earlier this fall, and counted separately for each of the three ways. No target yet

Signing up is the goal of this project. Whether students then use Athena matters too, because a jump in sign-ups from students who never use it isn't a win. The onboarding emails plan handles that part. These are Hinds students only. Community college students are more often part-time, working, or returning to school, so keep everything short and easy to read on a phone.

5 · Paid ads

Should we run ads?

Short answer: maybe, but not first, and not this week.

Hinds can reach exactly these students for free, inside Canvas and by email. Ads can't do that. Ads can only aim at an area (for example, around the Hinds campuses) and at interests, so some of the money would reach people who don't go to Hinds. Those people would land on our regular page, which offers the $5 trial. They also take time to set up and need a page to send people to, so a first test realistically fits the October 19 start, not this week.

You asked about pointing ads at specific areas. Two meanings apply. For where on the map: the area around the Hinds campuses and its online-student region. For where on our website: one page made for Hinds students, not the homepage.

If we do run a test

  • Send people to one page made for Hinds students. It should say it's free for them and tell them what to do next. Ask product whether this page exists or can be made in time.
  • Best first use: show ads to people who started the Hinds sign-up and didn't finish. Email can't reach them because they never made an account. This needs our ad tracking code on the website and a way to ask visitors for permission. Ask product whether both are in place.
  • Second use: a small ad campaign aimed at the area around the Hinds campuses, with a plain message: free for Hinds students.
  • Set a spending limit. Our channel-testing guide suggests about $1,000 over a few weeks. Judge it by how much each finished sign-up costs, and how much each student who actually uses Athena costs. Don't judge it by clicks.

Only run ads if all of these are true

  • A page made for Hinds students exists, or product can make one in time.
  • Hinds agrees we can use their name in ads.
  • We can tell which sign-ups came from ads and which came from elsewhere.
  • The numbers from step 1 show that students who open the sign-up page mostly finish it. If most quit, more visitors won't help. Agree on the cutoff with product.
  • Don't use Hinds student lists or data to choose who sees ads. Our public FAQ tells schools that nothing from them or from Canvas decides who sees an ad.
  • Don't run large-scale paid ads. Our current rule is no large-scale paid ads until the product is proven.

8 · After they sign up

The follow-up emails

This project gets students to sign up. The Hinds onboarding emails plan gets them from signed up to actually using Athena: connect Canvas, open a Study Space, then send a message, start a quiz, or start a tutoring session. It's one path for the student. Without the emails, more sign-ups can mean more students who never use it.

Where each student starts in the follow-up emails

Way they signed upWhere they startWhat the first email asks them to do
1. Main page with the school infoSecond stage. Already connected to Canvas and covered by Hinds.Open a Study Space.
2. Sign up normally, verify laterFirst stage. Not connected or covered yet.Click "Verify with Canvas."
3. Athena link inside CanvasProbably the second stage, like way 1. Product needs to confirm.Open a Study Space.

Catching up students who signed up earlier

When the follow-up emails start, every Hinds student who already has an account and is stuck gets one email, for the stage they're stuck at. They don't get the whole series at once.

So students who sign up this week and on October 19 get caught up when the emails launch, even if the emails aren't running yet. The onboarding emails plan calls this "backfill."

Where this can go wrong

  • Timing. None of the 9 emails are written, there's no way to send them yet, and there's no owner or launch date. A student who signs up on October 6 and hears from us weeks later has probably lost interest. Until then they only get the emails the app already sends.
  • Finding Hinds students. We find them by a Hinds email address or a Hinds tag on their account. Students who signed up with a personal email are invisible until they connect Canvas.
  • Not seeing who Hinds covers. Our analytics doesn't show it today. Until engineering adds it (it's the top request on the onboarding emails plan), the emails can't tell a covered student from one who connected Canvas but never verified.
  • Counting results. The follow-up emails are judged by how many students use Athena within 14 days of signing up. A student we catch up weeks later isn't a fair test of that, so count them separately. That's my recommendation. The onboarding emails plan doesn't say how to count them.

What I'd do: a small first version before October 19

Don't wait for all 9 emails. Send just two, plus the one-time catch-up:

  • The first email that tells students to open a Study Space.
  • The first email that tells them to try one thing in Athena: a quiz, a tutoring session, or a message.

These two don't depend on seeing who Hinds covers. Send them only to students who signed up with the school info (way 1, and way 3 if it counts the same way, which product needs to confirm), because for them we know Hinds is covering the cost. Don't say "free" to students we can't confirm are covered. Leave the first-stage emails (verify with Canvas) until product answers the charging question (question 4 above) and our analytics can see who's covered.

What the follow-up emails need this week

  • An owner and a launch date. Neither is set.
  • A way to send them: either our analytics tool sends them through our email service, or someone builds it inside the app. Engineering decides (question 7 above).
  • A decision on the emails the app already sends, such as the "Let's get to know each other" reminder, so a student never gets two emails in one day. The onboarding emails plan caps it at one email a day, including the app's own.
  • The two emails written and reviewed.
9 · What to say and not say

What to say and not say

Do

  • Say "free for Hinds students" or "Hinds is covering your access." Confirm the exact wording with Hinds.
  • Say it uses the same Hinds Canvas login students already use.
  • Keep it short and easy to read on a phone. One action, one link.
  • Use the approved terms and facts in reference/glossary.md and reference/messaging-framework.md.
  • Give each place we share it its own address, so we can see which one worked.

Don't

  • Use pressure, guilt, or "last chance."
  • Suggest that a Hinds email address alone gets free access. A current Canvas enrollment at Hinds does.
  • Suggest Athena is required, is part of Canvas, or is endorsed by Hinds beyond paying for it.
  • Say "FERPA compliant," make up results or student quotes, or share internal usage numbers.
  • Offer coupons, discounts, or dates. Don't describe the Athena link in Canvas to anyone outside the team until product confirms what it is.
