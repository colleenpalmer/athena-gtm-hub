## Market map
- **Direct competitors:** Consumer AI chatbots used for studying (e.g. ChatGPT free
  tier); generic study apps (e.g. Quizlet); upload-your-syllabus AI study tools.
- **Indirect / adjacent:** Self-built note systems (Notion, Google Docs/Keep); social
  gap-checking in Discord class chats; human tutoring and campus tutoring centers.
- **Status quo alternative:** "Do nothing" — re-reading scattered slides and notes, then
  cramming; pasting questions into a free chatbot.

## Competitor: ChatGPT (free tier)
- **Positioning (theirs):** General-purpose AI assistant for any question.
- **Strengths:** Free; ubiquitous; fast, clean answers; already a student habit.
- **Weaknesses / gaps:** Hands over answers students don't retain; no real course
  context (students paste content in); no mastery/gap tracking; ungoverned — institutions
  have zero visibility and it's where real IP/privacy leakage lives.
- **Where we win:** Coaches to the answer instead of giving it (retention); knows your
  actual Canvas courses automatically; tracks your gaps; read-only and doesn't train
  external models on your data.
- **Where we lose / risk:** Price (we're paid, it's free) and habit. Willingness to pay is
  our riskiest assumption — needs a high-stakes hook or a free/institutional path.
- **Pricing model (known):** Free tier (paid tiers exist; *needs research* for specifics).
- **Common traps they set:** "Why pay when this is free?"
- **Landmines (questions to plant):** "Does it know what's actually due in your classes
  this week?" "A month later, do you still understand it — or did you just copy it?"

## Competitor: Quizlet
- **Positioning (theirs):** Flashcards and study sets.
- **Strengths:** Free; familiar; large library of existing sets.
- **Weaknesses / gaps:** Generic and manual; not tied to your real course content;
  no coaching; no gap-tracking against your specific mastery.
- **Where we win:** Auto-built from your real Canvas materials; personalized to what
  you've mastered vs. where you're shaky; coaches understanding, not just recall drills.
- **Where we lose / risk:** Free and habitual; simple mental model ("just flashcards").
- **Pricing model (known):** Free with paid upgrade (*needs research* for specifics).
- **Landmines (questions to plant):** "Is your study set built from *your* lecture notes
  and this week's assignment — or someone else's generic set?"

## Competitor: Upload-your-syllabus AI study tools
- **Positioning (theirs):** AI study help personalized to your course — after you upload
  your syllabus/materials.
- **Strengths:** Some course personalization; purpose-built for studying.
- **Weaknesses / gaps:** Require manual upload/config; no live connection to the LMS;
  friction exactly when a stressed student's tolerance is lowest.
- **Where we win:** LMS-native auto-context (Study Spaces) — zero upload/config; the
  only study tutor that connects directly and securely to Canvas; institutional trust.
- **Where we lose / risk:** *Needs research* — specific named tools, feature depth, pricing.
- **Landmines (questions to plant):** "How much setup before it actually knows your
  classes — and does it stay current when your professor posts something new?"

## Our defensible differentiators (overall)
1. **The precision intersection — knows your class *and* knows you.** No competitor
   combines LMS-native course context with a rigorous personal mastery model.
2. **Only study tutor with a direct, secure Canvas connection** — zero-friction context,
   institutional trust, and a data moat competitors can't replicate (built by the company
   that runs the LMS).
3. **Principled coach-don't-tell** — efficiency toward real understanding and results,
   not disposable answers.
4. **Trust posture** — read-only, student-owned, revocable access; no training of external
   models on student data.
