# Traction Channels & Bullseye Framework

> Reference for growth marketing work. Summarizes the frameworks from *Traction: How Any
> Startup Can Achieve Explosive Customer Growth* (Gabriel Weinberg & Justin Mares).
> The frameworks are general-purpose; the **"Fit for us" notes are Athena-specific and
> should be kept current** as we learn from channel tests.

## Core ideas

- **Traction** = quantitative evidence of customer demand (growth in the metric that
  matters, not activity). Every channel effort must tie back to that metric.
- **The 50% rule** — spend ~50% of effort on product and ~50% on traction, from day one.
  Traction and product development are parallel, not sequential.
- **Traction thinking** — set a *traction goal* (the milestone that unlocks the next stage),
  then work back to the growth rate and channel mix needed to hit it.
- **Critical path** — the shortest set of milestones to the traction goal. Say no to
  everything not on it.
- **Underutilized channels win** — competitors crowd into obvious channels; the channel
  no one in your category is using is usually where the cheapest growth is.
- **Bullseye** — a repeatable process for finding the one channel that will move the
  needle *right now*. Most companies get most of their growth from a single channel at a
  time. Focus there; re-run Bullseye as you scale and the channel saturates.

## The Bullseye framework (5 steps)

| Step | Ring | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Brainstorm | Outer (all 19) | For **every** channel, write at least one plausible strategy for how it could work for us. Fight bias toward familiar channels. Note what competitors and adjacent companies do. |
| 2. Rank | — | Sort each channel into **A** (promising), **B** (possible), **C** (long shot). |
| 3. Prioritize | Middle (3 channels) | Pick the top 3 A's. If more than 3, pick the ones with the cheapest tests. |
| 4. Test | Middle | Run cheap, fast tests (~$1k / a few weeks max) in parallel. Answer: (a) what does a customer cost through this channel? (b) how many customers are available here? (c) are they the customers we want *right now*? |
| 5. Focus | Inner (1 channel) | Double down on the channel that showed traction. Wring it out with continual optimization until it saturates, then re-run Bullseye. |

Testing ≠ optimizing. Tests are about whether a channel works at all; optimization
(A/B, landing pages, offers) comes after you commit to a channel.

## The 19 traction channels

Grouped for scanning; the book presents them as a flat list of equals.

### Content & inbound
| # | Channel | What it is | Cheap first test | Key metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Targeting blogs** | Getting featured on blogs/newsletters/communities your target customer already reads (small, high-affinity ones first). | Guest post or sponsored mention on 3–5 niche blogs / student newsletters. | Referral signups per placement |
| 2 | **Publicity (PR)** | Traditional media coverage; work up the ladder from small outlets to large. | Pitch a data-backed story to 5 ed-tech / higher-ed trade outlets. | Coverage → referral traffic → signups |
| 3 | **Unconventional PR** | Publicity stunts and exceptional customer appreciation that earn attention. | One memorable, brand-true stunt or gesture with a built-in share mechanic. | Earned mentions, shares, signups |
| 4 | **Search engine marketing (SEM)** | Paid search ads on intent keywords. | $500–1k on 20–30 high-intent keywords with a dedicated landing page. | CPC, CTR, conversion, CAC |
| 5 | **Social & display ads** | Paid ads on social/display networks. | Small-budget tests on 2 platforms, 3 creatives each. | CPM, CTR, conversion, CAC |
| 6 | **Offline ads** | TV, radio, print, billboards, flyers, direct mail. | Flyers/posters in a single campus; unique URL or QR to track. | Cost per signup |
| 7 | **Search engine optimization (SEO)** | Ranking for search queries. *Fat-head* (few high-volume terms) vs *long-tail* (many specific terms). | Publish 10 long-tail pages targeting course/exam-specific queries; check indexing and rankings in 6–8 weeks. | Organic traffic, ranking, conversion |
| 8 | **Content marketing** | Owning a blog/resource that builds audience over time. Slow to start, compounds. | 4–6 pieces aimed at one persona's real questions; measure email captures. | Traffic, subscribers, assisted signups |
| 9 | **Email marketing** | Owned list for acquisition, activation, retention, and revenue. | Lifecycle sequence for trial users (welcome → activation → convert). | Open/click, activation rate, trial→paid |

### Product & viral
| # | Channel | What it is | Cheap first test | Key metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | **Engineering as marketing** | Building free tools, calculators, widgets, or micro-sites that attract your audience. | One free, useful micro-tool for the persona (e.g. a study planner) with a soft CTA. | Tool users → signups |
| 11 | **Viral marketing** | Existing users bring new users. Viral coefficient K = invites × conversion; also viral cycle time. | Add a share/invite moment at a natural high point; measure K. | K-factor, cycle time |

### Relationships & partnerships
| # | Channel | What it is | Cheap first test | Key metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | **Business development (BD)** | Partnerships that create value for both sides (distribution, co-marketing, integration, licensing). | 3–5 outreach conversations with complementary orgs (student orgs, success centers, tutoring providers). | Partner signups / seats |
| 13 | **Sales** | Direct selling — prospecting, qualifying, closing. Repeatable process over heroics. | Tight outbound sequence to 20–30 institutional prospects; measure meeting and pilot rates. | Meetings, pilot conversions, sales cycle |
| 14 | **Affiliate programs** | Paying partners a commission per customer. | Small referral program with student ambassadors or creators; unique codes. | Cost per acquisition, quality |
| 15 | **Existing platforms** | Riding the distribution of a platform with a large user base (app stores, marketplaces, LMS ecosystems, browser extensions). | Get listed / featured where the audience already is; optimize the listing. | Platform impressions → installs |

### Events & community
| # | Channel | What it is | Cheap first test | Key metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | **Trade shows** | Industry events where you exhibit or attend to meet buyers. | Attend (don't exhibit) one higher-ed conference; book 10 meetings in advance. | Qualified conversations, pilots |
| 17 | **Offline events** | Meetups, workshops, hosted events, sponsoring existing gatherings. | Host or sponsor one campus study session/workshop; track signups at the door. | Attendees → signups |
| 18 | **Speaking engagements** | Talks at conferences, webinars, campus events that build credibility and reach. | Pitch 3 talks/webinars on a topic the audience wants (not a product demo). | Audience reached → signups |
| 19 | **Community building** | Fostering a passionate user community that drives word of mouth and retention. | Seed a small, active group (Discord/campus cohort); measure engagement and referrals. | Active members, referrals, retention |

## Choosing channels by stage

The book's rule of thumb — channels that fit each phase of traction:

| Phase | Goal | Typical channel fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase I** — Making something people want | Get first users, learn fast | Sales, targeting blogs, offline events, community, unconventional PR, BD |
| **Phase II** — Marketing something people want | Find a scalable, repeatable channel | SEM, social ads, SEO, content, email, engineering as marketing, existing platforms, viral |
| **Phase III** — Scaling the business | Pour fuel on the winning channel; add a second | Everything that showed traction, at scale; offline ads, PR, trade shows |
