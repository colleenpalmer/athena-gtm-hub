# ICP & Buyer Personas

> Defines exactly who we sell to. Every campaign, message, and asset should map to someone here.
> Refine with the `icp-persona-development` skill. Sourced from `docs/`.

## Business model
Hybrid. **Primary = D2C to students.** Growing second motion = **institutions buying
seats** on behalf of students (primarily community colleges and undergraduate
universities). Future stretch: high-school (K–12) students later — the brand must not
feel so collegiate it can't grow down to a 16-year-old.

## Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

### Consumer ICP (the primary student)
- **Who:** Traditional undergraduate, 18–22, first or second year.
- **Course load:** 4–5 Canvas courses in a demanding or pre-professional track
  (pre-nursing, psych, bio, engineering) where GPA gates something concrete —
  grad school, a scholarship, program admission.
- **Ability:** Capable, not struggling.
- **Context:** Works part-time; studies in fragmented pockets; phone as much as laptop.
- **Money:** Steeped in free-tool culture and price-sensitive — leans on ChatGPT and
  Quizlet for free; won't pay for "general study help," only for something that clearly
  beats the free stack at a moment that matters.
- **Disqualifiers (bad fit):** Cheat-seekers who just want submission-ready answers (we
  cede them on purpose); students who want a generic answer engine, not coaching.

### Institutional ICP (the account)
- **Who:** A college, university, or department — usually a success center, advising
  office, or Canvas admin — evaluating or paying for AI study tools on behalf of students.
- **Segment:** Community colleges and undergraduate universities.
- **Trigger events (why now):** Students already using ChatGPT and similar tools
  ungoverned; leadership starting to ask what the institution's stance is; IP/privacy risk.
- **What they want to avoid:** unbounded per-student cost; student work or course IP
  exposed to a model that trains on it; another disconnected tool that doesn't talk to Canvas.
- **Disqualifiers:** Institutions not on Canvas (the Canvas connection is the core hook).

## Personas

### Persona 1: The capable-but-scattered undergrad (primary, D2C)
- **Role & seniority:** Student, 18–22, first/second year, pre-professional track.
- **Primary goal:** Do better in the classes that matter and protect the GPA that gates
  their next step — without wasting time.
- **Top pains:**
  - Can't see their own gaps (confuse "I've seen this" with "I know this").
  - No durable study system — intentions decay into cramming.
  - Material scattered across slides, notes, and Canvas.
  - Current tools solve only a slice (ChatGPT hands over answers they don't retain;
    Quizlet is generic and manual).
- **Emotional baseline:** A low, persistent "am I missing something?" that spikes to
  panic 1–2 days before exams. Friction tolerance drops as stress rises.
- **What they care about when buying:** Does it just work? Does it clearly beat the free
  stack right now? Low stakes to try, low price.
- **Objections we hear:** "Why pay when ChatGPT/Quizlet are free?" (Willingness to pay
  is the riskiest assumption — needs a high-stakes hook or a free/institutional path.)
- **Where they hang out:** Canvas (checked reflexively), ChatGPT, Quizlet, TikTok,
  Instagram, Reddit, Discord (class group chats — where gap-checking happens socially),
  Notion / Google Docs, Spotify (study playlists).
- **Role in the deal:** User and economic buyer (for individual subscriptions).

### Persona 2: The institutional sponsor (adjacent)
- **Role & seniority:** Success center lead, advising office, or Canvas admin;
  leadership sponsor for the budget.
- **Primary goal:** Give students real study support responsibly — without unbounded
  cost, IP/privacy exposure, or yet another disconnected tool.
- **Top pains:** Ungoverned student AI use with zero visibility; IP leakage and privacy
  risk from students pasting course content into consumer chatbots; pressure to take a
  stance.
- **What they care about when buying:** Security and privacy (read-only, no model
  training), Canvas integration, academic integrity, bounded cost, a partner they can call.
- **Objections we hear:** "Can students connect to our Canvas without our approval?"
  "How do we opt out / see who's connected?" "Is this FERPA compliant?"
  (See `docs/Athena & Canvas FAQ Internal.pdf` for approved answers.)
- **Where they hang out:** InstructureCon and the Instructure/Canvas ecosystem;
  higher-ed advising and student-success communities.
- **Role in the deal:** Economic buyer / champion (sponsored subscriptions and pilots).

## Persona commonalities (the strength)
- **Same root problem.** Students are already on ungoverned AI; institutions are anxious
  about it. One fix closes both gaps.
- **Same Canvas connection, two payoffs.** Frictionless for the student; secure for the
  institution.
- **Same "doesn't train on your material" fact, two payoffs.** Retention for the student;
  IP protection for the institution.
- **Coach-don't-tell.** Real understanding for the student; integrity story for the institution.
- **Gap-tracking is early signal for both.** Personal for the student; advising-relevant
  for the institution.

## Jobs To Be Done
- **The single JTBD (student):** *When I sit down to study, I want to be told the right thing
  to work on next — for me, right now — so I stop wasting time and walk in ready.*
- When it's late and I'm stuck on a problem, I want to be coached to the answer, so I
  actually understand it instead of just copying it.
- When exams pile up, I want to know what I don't know and what to do about it, so my
  intentions don't decay into cramming.
- (Institution) When students are using AI ungoverned, I want a safe, sanctioned,
  Canvas-connected option, so I reduce IP/privacy risk without an unbounded cost.
