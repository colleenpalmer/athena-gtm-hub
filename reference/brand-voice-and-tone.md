# Brand Voice & Tone

> How we sound. Every piece of copy should pass these checks.
> Enforced via the always-apply rule; used heavily by `content-brief` and `messaging-review`.
> Sourced from `docs/` (Creative Brand Brief, Athena Playbook v1).

## Voice in a sentence
Athena is a **warm expert coach** — the brilliant person who makes *you* feel smart:
calm, credible, and steadily confident at the 11pm-before-an-exam moment
("I've got you, I know the drill, I'll guide you through this").

## Archetype
The **warm expert coach** — a step ahead but never showing off; you're relieved to have
them because you never doubt their competence. **Explicitly NOT the peer study-buddy**
(the classmate who may or may not know the answer), and NOT a stodgy professor.

## Voice attributes (we are / we are not)
| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Approachable | Cold or clinical |
| Authentic | Hype-y or salesy ("more marketing, less tool") |
| "Humble brightness" — clever but not show-offy | Know-it-all / condescending |
| Clear, confident, conversational | Jargon-heavy or wordy |
| Warm and supportive | Saccharine, cutesy, or "cringe" |
| Calm and credible | Urgent, shaming, or nagging |
| Solid > clever | Trend-chasing wordplay |

Aligned with the Instructure house voice: knowledgeable-not-know-it-all,
encouraging-not-patronizing, playful-not-juvenile.

## Tone by context
- **Website / product:** Calm, crafted utility — competent and frictionless before flashy.
  "More tool, less marketing."
- **Sales / enablement (institutional):** Credible and reassuring; lead with security,
  privacy, integration, and partnership. This is the register where learning science and
  "powered by IgniteAI" live.
- **Social:** Friendly, calm, supportive; meet students at the moment they feel stuck so
  it reads as relief, not a pitch. A hook can lean into urgency when the platform calls
  for it, but the body stays clear and confident, never pushy.
- **Support / lifecycle email:** Like a coach checking in — warm, personal, a little
  celebratory when it's earned; never a company "retaining a subscriber."
- **Executive / analyst / PR:** Confident and principled; explain the student-authorized
  model well, answer every question, don't apologize or overclaim.

## The celebration discipline — "applaud mastery, not attendance"
Keep streaks, the occasional emoji, and "learning moments" — but every celebration
must be **specific, earned, and credible**, tied to demonstrated competence, not activity
or login frequency. The moment it rewards attendance over mastery, it has slid back into
study-buddy.

## Delight calibration
Mostly "it just worked" delight (quiet, competent smoothness) with a light sprinkle of
personality. When they conflict, **frictionless wins** — charm is seasoning, never the meal.

## Style rules
- **Point of view:** Talk to the student as "you"; active voice.
- **Lead with the benefit / results** for students; save the mechanism and learning
  science for the institutional register.
- **Concise:** short sentences, one idea at a time; bullets over walls of text.
- **Reading level:** accessible to an ~18–22-year-old undergrad; must stretch down
  toward high school later without feeling too collegiate.
- **AI posture:** AI is the engine, never the badge — don't lead with "AI-powered" in
  student-facing copy.

## Words & phrases
- **Use:** students (working audience word), study coach, coach you to the answer,
  know your courses / know you, no wasted effort, walk in ready, Study Spaces,
  future-ready.
- **Avoid (house banned words):** harness, unlock, leverage, empower, future-proof.
- **Avoid (playbook "language to avoid"):** crush it, grind, actually, falling behind,
  as I mentioned, let's see if, that's not quite right, drill me, dominate, hustle, killing it.
- **Never say (legal/brand no-gos):** "FERPA compliant" or "FERPA certified" (no such
  certification); don't promise dashboards, admin controls, or specific dates; never
  present Athena as part of Canvas, endorsed by a school, or required for coursework.

## Do / Don't
- **Do:** be concise, warm, credible; explain *why* something's worth knowing; protect
  the learner's dignity; allow productive friction when it's the right call.
- **Don't:** lecture pedagogy at students; be saccharine, hype, or cringe; nag; reward
  vanity metrics; ever talk down.

## Emotional territory
- **Dominant note:** capability / earned progress — students leave *more capable* and
  holding the credit.
- **On first contact (priority order):** relief → clarity → "it just worked" delight.
- **FEEL:** trust, relief, encouraged, guided, supported, safe (vulnerable, not judged),
  "I'm smarter because of it," clarity, progress, respected.
- **NEVER feel (cardinal third rail):** **judged / made to feel dumb.** Secondary
  never-feel: **confused.** Also avoid: attacked, lost, discouraged, bored, slowed down,
  overwhelmed, condescended to, "AI slop," nagged, repetitive.
- **Principle:** make them do the work; never make them feel small.

## Examples
- ✅ On-brand: "Okay, here's where to start." / "You've got this down — let's spend your
  time on the two concepts that are still shaky." / "Let's try that again."
- ❌ Off-brand: "You're out of time!" (shaming/urgent) · "Let's see if you got it" (tests,
  doesn't coach) · "That's not quite right" (deflating) · "Unlock your potential with
  AI-powered studying" (banned words + AI-as-badge + hype).

## The gut-test
*Would a student feel solid — not cheap, not embarrassed — recommending this to a
friend?* If no, it doesn't meet the bar, no matter how clever it is.
